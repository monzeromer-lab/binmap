binmap

A source-aware binary analysis tool for Rust.

It reads your source and your compiled binary at the same time, and uses that dual view to explain why your program is wrong, why it is slow, and why it is large.

Status: pre-alpha. Nothing works yet. Linux x86-64 · Apache-2.0 or MIT

Fixed vocabulary

Measured, derived, inferred

● Measured

A tool ran. The command, its arguments and a digest of its output are all recorded.

◈ Derived

One of our own rules reached a conclusion from measured inputs. The rule is named.

◆ Inferred

A model said so. Capped at Probable, and it may only cite evidence the engine issued.

The interface never shows one without the other. Running with no reasoner connected is a first-class setting, and the whole test suite runs that way.

Written down and binding

Non-goals

Recording these matters more than recording the goals. A solo project dies from scope creep.
N1 Not an IDE. It reads your code; you edit elsewhere.
N2 Not a linter. If Clippy can find it, binmap does not try.
N3 Not cross-platform in v1. Linux x86-64 only.
N4 Not a replacement for GDB. It drives debuggers; it is not one.
N5 Not a hosted service. No accounts, no server, no telemetry.
N7 No autonomous commits, and never as the default.

Where contributions land

The analysis engines

Attribution, demangling, unwinding, register recovery. Deterministic, testable, and the part that decides whether the product is any good.

The artifact format

binmap.json carries findings, evidence and gate results. Schema work here unblocks every consumer, including CI dashboards nobody has written yet.

Tool adapters

Every backend reads someone else's output format, and every one of those formats drifts. Snapshot fixtures and version probes are self-contained tasks.
